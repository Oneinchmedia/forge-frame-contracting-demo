The One Storyboard

Storyboard · process film · One Inch Media · how a website gets built

The One

A forty-five-second film that takes a client from a tired template to a live site, start to finish, through the green rain and out the other side.

  • 45 s
  • 16:9 · 24 fps
  • 12 frames
  • 10 s cutdown
  • Matrix homage, no likeness
  • Built on the real lockup
  • Green only in the old world

How this one works

  • The subject is the process. Audit, the choice, a design system, learning the brand, design, code, review, QA, deploy, results. Every frame is a real step in how a site gets built, and every step names the AI that does the heavy lifting.
  • The film is the lens. The old site is the simulation, rendered in green rain. The brand red is the choice. The blank white room is the canvas. The rain reversed is the code being written. Bullet time is the design review. The agents are the bugs. And the One is your 1.
  • Color tells the story. Green exists only in the old world and on the bugs. The moment the client chooses, the piece is black, bone and red for good. A version with no green at all is possible; the green is the recognizable signal.
  • Still open: the client to feature (the Forge & Frame demo site in this repo is a ready candidate, with its photos), the real results numbers, the fonts, and the voice. Everything shown in the real-world frame is a placeholder until then.

Structure

Four acts that follow the real order of a build. The green dies at nine seconds and never comes back except on the bugs.

I · 0:00–0:09

The old world

A terminal wakes the client up, the template site dissolves into rain, and two buttons appear. One is red.

II · 0:09–0:21

The construct

A white room, racks of components, the brand loaded in seconds, and a sparring session that tightens the design.

III · 0:21–0:33

The build

The rain reversed: code rising into a page. A frozen review in bullet time. Four agents, four strikes.

IV · 0:33–0:45

Launch

One command, live on every device, the real world in daylight, and the One.

The board

Twelve frames. Each names the step in the build, the AI capability it proves, and how the shot would be produced. Timings sum to forty-five seconds exactly.

010:00 – 0:03 · 3 s

Wake up

Visual
A black terminal. A green cursor blinks. Text types itself, one character at a time: Wake up. A beat. Your website is from 2016. Scanlines, a soft green bloom, the faint curve of an old monitor.
Motion
Locked off. Typing at 18 characters a second with human-looking jitter. The cursor blinks at 1 Hz.
Step
The audit. The first line is the diagnosis.
Audio
A key click per character. Room hum. A low drone starts under the second line.
AI
An audit agent crawls the client's existing site, scores it (performance, accessibility, copy, structure) and writes the one-line diagnosis. The typing is that agent's real output, not a script.
Reference
The terminal wake-up, by form. Two words that any screen could say; the second line is ours.
Out
The text falls apart into glyphs and starts to rain.
020:03 – 0:06 · 3 s

The old site

Visual
Green rain. Behind it, the template: a generic hero with a stock-photo placeholder, three identical columns, a footer nobody reads, all drawn as a green wireframe. The rain falls through it and the wireframe dissolves line by line. The glyphs are not the film's: they are HTML tags, CSS tokens and the numeral 1.
Motion
Slow push-in, 1.0× to 1.15×. The rain runs at three speeds in three depths.
Step
Mapping what exists.
Audio
The rain as a soft hiss of clicks. The drone holds.
AI
Site ingestion: an agent reads every page, every image and every line of copy into a structured brief in seconds. The rain is that read, visualized: the old site becoming data.
Reference
The digital rain, by image. Our own glyph set, no katakana.
Out
The last of the wireframe goes. Two buttons fade up.
030:06 – 0:09 · 3 s

The choice

Visual
Two buttons on black. Left, a green outline: KEEP THE TEMPLATE. Right, a solid red block, the brand's first appearance: BUILD IT RIGHT. Under the red one, small: 6 pages · 10 days · one team. A cursor drifts, hovers, clicks red. The green button and the last rain die to black. The red holds.
Motion
The red button pops in with overshoot. The cursor takes a full second to decide. The click is a one-frame flash to bone.
Step
The scope and the decision.
Audio
The drone thins. One click. Silence as the green goes.
AI
Scoping: from the brief, an agent proposes the page list, the timeline and the estimate shown under the red button. The decision itself stays human; the piece makes a point of the cursor's pause.
Reference
The choice, by color only. No pills, no hands. Two buttons on a screen.
Out
Cut to white.
040:09 – 0:13 · 4 s

The construct

Visual
An infinite white room. The 1 stands alone, small, black. Then racks slide in from the horizon on both sides, faster and faster: navigation bars, heroes, cards, forms, footers, tables, galleries, every component in black line, labeled. Top of frame: WE NEED COMPONENTS.
Motion
The racks arrive on a curve, slow then fast, each overshooting slightly before it settles. The 1 does not move.
Step
The design system.
Audio
Silence, then a rack whoosh per component, a clean synth pulse underneath.
AI
Generative design system: from the brief and the brand kit, an agent produces a complete component library in the client's brand in one pass, tokens included. The racks are that library, rendered.
Reference
The loading construct, by concept. The line is a paraphrase, not a quotation.
Out
White snaps to black.
050:13 – 0:17 · 4 s

Loaded

Visual
Black. The 1, large, center. The red slash cuts through it and stays: the jack. Lines of mono text stream in from the left and vanish into the numeral: LOADING brand voice, LOADING 212 photos, LOADING type and spacing, LOADING tone. A red bar fills. Then, in the wordmark's spaced caps: I KNOW YOUR BRAND.
Motion
Each line accelerates into the 1 and is gone. The bar fills in two seconds. The last line lands hard.
Step
Learning the brand.
Audio
A data-stream texture, rising. A chime on the bar. A dry hit on the line.
AI
Brand learning: voice, photography and typographic habits distilled from everything the client has ever published, in seconds, into a style the agents write and design against. The piece says it plainly.
Reference
The upload, by idea. The line is a paraphrase and it is your call.
Out
Hard cut to the dojo.
060:17 – 0:21 · 4 s

The sparring program

Visual
A bare wooden floor, planks converging. The 1 on the left, leaning. On the right, the new site's wireframe stands up like an opponent, slightly loose: boxes a few pixels off, lines not quite level. Three strikes. Each one snaps something tight: the grid, the type scale, the spacing. Red guide lines flash on every hit. Top left: DESIGN · grid 12 · 8 px · scale 1.25.
Motion
Locked wide. Three strikes on three beats; the wireframe jitter reduces to zero by the third.
Step
Design, and the designer's corrections.
Audio
Wood floor. Three strikes, three snaps. The pulse continues.
AI
Layout generation with a human in the loop: the agent proposes the page, the designer strikes, the agent re-flows. Three rounds, under a minute. The strikes are the human beat of the piece.
Reference
The sparring program, and the dojo is the Bruce Lee wink the other two boards carry. A wooden floor, nothing more.
Out
The wireframe, now tight, falls away into glyphs that start to rise.
070:21 – 0:25 · 4 s

The rain, reversed

Visual
The rain again, but rising, in bone with red heads. This is code being written, not read. As the glyphs climb they assemble a page from the bottom up: footer, sections, hero, navigation, each locking in as the rain passes it. The camera tilts up with the build.
Motion
Continuous tilt-up over four seconds. Sections lock on beat subdivisions.
Step
The build.
Audio
The rain reversed as a rising arpeggio. A lock click per section.
AI
Code generation: components to production code, typed, tested and committed by an agent. The rain is the literal diff from the client's build streaming past, so the effect is real data.
Reference
The rain, inverted. Ours rises.
Out
Freeze.
080:25 – 0:29 · 4 s

Bullet time

Visual
The page freezes mid-build and the camera orbits it. Its layers separate in depth: background, hero image, headline, button, navigation, each a plane hanging in black. Red annotations appear in the frozen space with leader lines: CONTRAST 4.6:1, LCP 1.2 S, COPY: SHORTER. Time resumes, the planes snap flat, the notes become checks.
Motion
A 120° orbit over three seconds, ease in and out, then a one-frame snap back to flat.
Step
Review.
Audio
A time-stretch whoosh into near silence. Pen scratches for the notes. A snap.
AI
Automated review: accessibility, performance and copy checks run on the real page and annotated in 3D, then fixed by the agent. The layers are the actual DOM, captured as depth, so the orbit is a real camera move through a real page.
Reference
Bullet time, by technique. No bodies, no bullets.
Out
Snap to the corridor.
090:29 – 0:33 · 4 s

Agents

Visual
A corridor. The old world fights back: four black blocks advance, labeled in green: SAFARI 15, 3.8 S LOAD, BROKEN FORM, 404 × 12. The 1 takes them one at a time, a strike each, and every block becomes a bone check that recedes. Top left: QA · AGENTS 4/4.
Motion
The blocks scale up from the vanishing point; each strike is a slash flash and a one-frame shake. Four beats.
Step
QA.
Audio
Footsteps. Four strikes, four dings.
AI
QA agents: cross-browser runs, performance budgets, form tests and link checks, each finding its own bug and fixing it. The word agents means both things at once, and the piece enjoys that.
Reference
The agents, by name only. Blocks with labels, no suits, no faces.
Out
The last check recedes. Cut to the terminal.
100:33 – 0:37 · 4 s

Deploy

Visual
The terminal from frame 01, but in bone now, the prompt in red. $ deploy. building · 212 files. A red bar. DEPLOY? [y/N] y. Then, large, in spaced caps: LIVE. Devices pop in on the right, a phone, a laptop, a billboard, each with the new site inside.
Motion
Typing, a two-second bar, a hard landing on LIVE. The devices pop with overshoot on three beats.
Step
Launch.
Audio
Keys. A build whoosh. One hit on LIVE. Three pops.
AI
One-command deployment: build, DNS, certificates, CDN and device previews, all automated. The y is the last thing a human types.
Reference
The exit, by idea. A prompt, not a phone.
Out
Cut to daylight.

What the piece proves about AI

One capability per step of a real build, in the order a client would meet them. The film doubles as the service explained.

  • F01Audit agent: crawl, score and diagnose an existing site in one line.
  • F02Site ingestion: every page, image and sentence into a structured brief.
  • F03Scoping: page list, timeline and estimate proposed; the decision stays human.
  • F04Generative design system: a full component library in the brand, tokens included.
  • F05Brand learning: voice, photography and type habits distilled in seconds.
  • F06Layout generation with a human in the loop: propose, strike, re-flow.
  • F07Code generation: components to production code, typed, tested, committed.
  • F08Automated review: accessibility, performance and copy checks, annotated and fixed.
  • F09QA agents: browsers, budgets, forms and links, each bug found and fixed.
  • F10One-command deployment with device previews.
  • F11Analytics agent: results reported, optimization continued.
  • F12Procedural logo animation shared across all three films.

Guardrails and your calls

What the piece deliberately avoids so the homage is safe to run commercially, and the decisions only you can make.

What we do not do

  • No characters. No faces, no sunglasses, no coats, no bodies in black. The only figure is your numeral.
  • No dialogue. Nothing on screen is a quotation. "Wake up" is two generic words; the construct line, the upload line and the spoon line are paraphrases in the studio's own words.
  • No katakana. The rain's glyphs are HTML tags, CSS tokens and the 1.
  • No pills, no phone, no title. The choice is two buttons, the exit is a prompt, and the film's name appears nowhere.
  • No film audio. No score, no effects, nothing sampled.
  • Green is quarantined. The old world and the bugs only. Never on the lockup, never after the choice.
  • Bruce Lee stays a wink. The dojo is a wooden floor and three strikes; no likeness, no costume, no line.

Decisions for you

  1. The three paraphrases. "We need components.", "I know your brand." and "There is no template." are the lines closest to the film's. Keep all, some, or none.
  2. Green or no green. The green is the recognizable signal. A version in brand colors only still works; the rain would be steel.
  3. The client. Feature the Forge & Frame demo, a real client, or a fictional one.
  4. Length. Forty-five seconds for the site and pitches; the ten-second cut for social. A thirty-second middle cut is possible by dropping frames 02, 06 and 09.
  5. The voice. Your own voice cloned (consent and a short recording), a cast synthetic voice, or none.
  6. The numbers. The results in frame 11 must be real before the film ships, or the ticker is cut.

Other directions considered

Three ways to tell the same process that lost to the rain, and what each one still lends.

The glitch

The whole film as a déjà vu loop: the old site keeps repeating until the 1 breaks the loop. Clever, but it spends twenty seconds on the problem. It survives as the four agents in frame 09.

Operator

Told entirely from the operator's screen: a wall of terminals watching the build happen. Very on-brand for a studio, but it keeps the viewer outside the work. It survives as the terminal bookends in frames 01 and 10.

Red pill, literally

The client swallows the red and wakes up inside the finished site. The most quotable, and the one most likely to read as a copy of the scene rather than a nod. Set aside; the two buttons carry the idea.
