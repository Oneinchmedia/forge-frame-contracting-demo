The Arena

Forty seconds through the eyes of a brand: three bouts in a stick-figure arena, a crowd that decides, a throne of old logos, and the one thing a refresh earns you back.

  • 40 s
  • 16:9 · 24 fps
  • 10 frames
  • 8 s cutdown
  • First person, no blood, nobody falls
  • Built on the real lockup
  • Stick figures

What changed and why

  • The war is scrapped. Its dawn frame put rows of identical figures with raised arms under a red banner with a round emblem. That composition reads as rally imagery whatever the copy says, and it is gone from the artifact gallery and the repo.
  • This one has no armies, no flags, no salutes. One hero, three opponents, a stadium crowd that is uneven and loud, and a camera that is the hero's eyes. The rule is written into the board: nothing marches, nothing is held over a crowd, nobody lines up.
  • The argument is the same. A brand fights for attention in public. The opponents are what a stale brand faces: its own 2019 self, the template it settled for, and the algorithm that stopped liking it. The crowd decides with a thumb. A refresh is how you earn the thumb again.
  • The flavors are the arena and the throne. Gladiator gives the sand, the gate, the stands and the thumb. The other show gives the long first-person take through a fight and a throne made of what you conquered. Ours is made of old logos.

The POV

The camera is the hero. You see the arm, the slash, the sand and whatever is coming at you. The loop runs one bout: approach, strike, refresh.

  • The armOne bone arm from the bottom right, swaying with the walk, holding the slash as a blade. It rises when something closes in and drops when the fight is done.
  • The strikeThe slash sweeps right to left across the whole frame in nineteen frames with a red smear behind it. It is the only weapon in the film and it never cuts anyone: it refreshes them.
  • The cameraDodges, drops and spins. A sidestep is a lateral shove and a 6° roll. Going low for the template is a 22% drop of the horizon. The algorithm spins you once around.
  • OpponentsThree, each a shape: your 2019 self with a pixel shield, the template as a tower of lorem bars with a stock-photo head, the algorithm as a figure with six arms holding thumbs-down signs.
  • The crowdUneven on purpose. Random heights, random gestures (waves, claps, one arm up, none), random timing. It jumps when it likes something. It never lines up.
  • The thumbA sign in the box: a line for undecided, a chevron down, a chevron up in red. It is a like, not a sentence.

The bout

Visual
One take. We sidestep: the world shoves right and rolls, and the shield swings through where we were, a grey smear. We strike: the slash sweeps the frame right to left and the shield bursts into pixels. The figure fills in bone, bows, and its label changes to REFRESHED. The crowd jumps.

Sidestep in seven frames, 14% of frame width, a 6° roll. Shield pass in six frames with motion smear. Strike in nineteen frames. Bow over a second. Crowd jump on the last beat.
Audio
A whoosh past the ear. The hit. Pixels scattering like gravel. The crowd.
AI
The POV take is a generated camera path solved against the opponent's motion so the dodge is physically plausible; the shield burst is rigid-body; the refresh is a per-character state change.

Guardrails and your calls

The rules this board is built under, including the one the last board broke. Then the decisions.

What we do not do

  • No rally imagery. No rows of figures, no uniform gesture, no raised arms in unison, no flags, no emblem held over a crowd, no leader addressing a mass. The crowd is uneven, loud and random. This is the rule the last board broke.
  • No blood, nobody falls. The strike refreshes. The thumb is a like. Every opponent is standing and bone at the end.
  • No likeness, no quotes. No characters, costumes, names or lines from either film or show. No emperor, no helmet, no sigils, no dragons, no theme music.
  • No swords in the throne. Ours is made of old logos, and the hero refuses to sit in it.
  • No real brands. The opponents are the client's own past and generic categories, never a competitor.
  • Original audio only. Everything generated or recorded for this piece.

Decisions for you

  1. The four lines. "Every brand fights for attention." "The crowd decides." "And the crowd turns." "You don't keep the crowd. You earn it again." Keep, cut, or voice-over.
  2. The slash as a blade. The board carries it as the only weapon and it never cuts. If even that is too much for a client, the hero can strike open-handed and the slash can be the light that follows.
  3. Which opponents. 2019 you, the template, the algorithm are the proposal. A client version could swap one for their real pain point.
  4. Specific or generic. The shield, the throne and the template built from a real client's old brand, or kept generic for the studio reel.
  5. The service line. "Brand refresh" under the lockup, or nothing.
  6. The voice. One line on the lap in your voice, a cast voice, or none.

Other directions considered

Two other arenas that lost to this one, and the one that was scrapped.

The tournament

A bracket of eight brands with the client climbing it. Clear structure, but it spends the film on other people's logos and it needs a loser in every round. The three-bout version keeps every opponent standing.

The training yard

No crowd: the brand drills alone before the fight, and the arena is only glimpsed at the end. Quieter and cheaper, but the argument lives in the crowd, and the crowd deciding is the whole point.

The war (scrapped)

Two stick-figure armies and a refreshed line. The dawn frame read as rally imagery, and no edit of the copy fixes a composition. Replaced by this board, whose first rule is that nothing lines up.
