Storyboard · brand ident · One Inch Media · v1

One Inch

A thirty-second ident that measures one inch of air, closes it, and lets the force travel through every medium AI can touch.

  • 30 s
  • 16:9 · 24 fps
  • 10 frames
  • 7 s cutdown
  • Bruce Lee homage, no likeness
  • Logo, colors and type are slots

Assumptions, because the brand kit was not reachable

  • The brand is One Inch Media. The name is the Bruce Lee reference: the one-inch punch. The whole piece is built on that distance. If the brand is something else, the spine still works, but the lockup gag in frame 09 is specific to this name.
  • iCloud Drive is not connected to this session, so the logo, colors and fonts here are placeholders. Every place the real kit drops in is listed under Brand slots. The yellow is a proposal, chosen because it reads as the reference without any costume or stripe.
  • The animatic is a rough previz, drawn live in the browser. It shows timing, composition and motion. It is not the generated footage the frames describe.

Structure

Four movements. Stillness, one hit, the force traveling, and the name. The edit rhythm itself is the homage: long quiet, then everything at once.

I · 0:00–0:09

The inch

A measurement, a gap, a breath. Nothing is generated yet that the viewer can tell. The piece earns the hit by waiting.

II · 0:09–0:13

The hit

One frame of white, a shockwave through steel, and an empty chair sliding back across a studio floor.

III · 0:13–0:25

The force travels

The wave passes through a photograph, a flat logo, a page of text and a wireframe, and upgrades each. Then ten thousand takes, and one.

The board

Ten frames. Each names the AI capability it proves and how the shot would be produced. Timings sum to thirty seconds exactly.

010:00 – 0:03 · 3 s

The tick

Visual
Black. Dead center, a single hairline tick the height of a ruler's inch mark fades up. Beside it, small mono: 1 in. Fine grain so the black breathes. Nothing else.
Motion
Locked off. The tick arrives over twenty frames, never on a cut. No camera.
On screen
1 in
Audio
Room tone only, matched to the studio we land in at frame 05. A sub-audible 40 Hz bed starts at 0:02.
AI
Generative sound design: the room tone and bed are synthesized from a description of the space, or from the scan of the real studio if we capture it. The first three seconds deliberately contain nothing visual that AI made. The piece starts with a measurement.
Out
Hold. The tick becomes one mark of the ruler in frame 02, a match cut.
020:03 – 0:07 · 4 s

One inch of air

Visual
Macro. Two machined steel blocks fill the left and right of frame, brushed finish, cool highlights. Between them: exactly one inch of air. A ruler resolves along the bottom around the tick. Dust drifts through a blade of light cutting the gap diagonally. Readout under the gap: 1.000 in.
Motion
Slow push into the gap, 1.0× to 1.25× over four seconds, ease-in. The dust moves on its own physics.
On screen
1.000 in
Audio
Room tone. One soft tick at 0:03 as the ruler resolves. The bed rises slowly.
AI
Photoreal text-to-video with camera-path control. The steel, the brushed-metal anisotropy and the dust in volumetric light are generated, with the camera move specified numerically so it carries straight into frame 03 without a cut.
Out
The push continues into frame 03. No cut.
030:07 – 0:09 · 2 s

The breath

Visual
We are inside the inch. The two steel faces are the left and right edges of frame. The motes hang nearly still. A faint ring breathes at center. The readout counts down: 1.000, 0.750, 0.500, 0.250.
Motion
Time stretches to the equivalent of 1000 fps. The blocks close slowly at first, then the last quarter inch vanishes in two frames.
On screen
The countdown readout.
Audio
Everything drops out except a heartbeat at 48 bpm. Last beat at 0:08.9.
AI
Generative frame interpolation (temporal super-resolution): 1000 fps-equivalent slow motion synthesized from 24 fps, with the motes moving correctly in three dimensions rather than crossfading.
Out
Smash cut to white on the frame the gap reaches zero.
040:09 – 0:10 · 1 s

The hit

Visual
One frame of white. Then black, and a shockwave: concentric rings expanding from the seam where the blocks met, steel debris as fine sparks, radial speed lines. The seam stays, a hairline of light.
Motion
Rings expand with exponential ease-out. Hard screen shake for three frames. Everything is over by 0:10.
On screen
None.
Audio
One dry hit, no reverb. Then a long metallic ring, a struck steel plate, that decays under everything for the next twenty seconds.
AI
Neural physics simulation: the shock propagating through steel with real material properties (density, elastic modulus) drives the ring deformation and the spark release. Simulated and rendered, not keyframed.
Out
Cut on the second ring to the wide shot.
050:10 – 0:13 · 3 s

The chair

Visual
Wide. Polished concrete floor in a bare, well-lit studio. A single empty wooden chair, side on. On the hit's echo it skids backward across the floor and stops. Nobody is in frame. A dimension line draws along the floor as it travels: 8 ft 2 in.
Motion
Camera locked, 35 mm, chest height. The chair launches with exponential ease-out and a four-degree yaw. Dust lifts from the floor and settles after it stops.
On screen
8 ft 2 in
Audio
Chair legs on concrete, dust. The steel ring continues. Bed holds.
AI
Physics-consistent generative video with object control: the chair's path is specified, the model renders contact, dust and floor reflection. The room is a Gaussian-splat capture of the real One Inch studio if we scan it (a brand slot), otherwise generated.
Reference
A nod to the 1964 Long Beach demonstration, where the volunteer's landing chair became part of the legend. No person is shown.
Out
The camera begins a dolly right that carries into frame 06.
060:13 – 0:18 · 5 s

Through every medium

Visual
The dolly reveals a row of panels standing on the studio floor: a printed photograph on foam board, a flat vinyl logo on the wall, a page of printed text, a wireframe model on a plinth. A translucent band of force sweeps left to right through them. Each panel transforms as the band passes: the photograph starts moving, the flat logo lifts off the wall as machined metal, the text lifts off the page and is spoken, the wireframe renders photoreal.
Motion
One continuous dolly, five seconds, no cuts. The band moves slightly faster than the camera, so it overtakes each panel in turn.
On screen
None. The text panel's words are heard, not read.
Audio
The band's passage is a low whoosh. Each transformation gets one quiet, specific sound: shutter, metal, breath, render chime. A synthesized voice reads the page: Everything within reach.
AI
Four capabilities in one shot: image-to-video (the photograph), 2D-to-3D lift with material inference (the logo), text-to-speech (the page), and wireframe-to-photoreal re-rendering (the model). The continuous shot is the proof that these are one pipeline, not four demos.
Out
The band hits the far wall and the wall lights up.
070:18 – 0:22 · 4 s

Ten thousand takes

Visual
The far wall is a grid of small screens, each playing a different take of the chair shot: another lens, another time of day, another floor, some vertical, some square, some wide. They fill the wall faster and faster. A counter climbs from 0 to 10,000.
Motion
Slow push-in. The tiles populate in a wave from the band's impact point.
On screen
10,000 TAKES
Audio
A dense, dry pulse builds, one click per hundred takes, accelerating.
AI
Batch generation at scale with controlled variation (seeded, so every tile is genuinely different) plus automatic reframing into vertical, square and wide. In the web version the wall renders live, so no two viewers see the same ten thousand.
Out
The counter stops on 10,000 and the wall goes silent.
080:22 – 0:25 · 3 s

One

Visual
Every tile flies to the center and goes dark except one. That one fills the frame: the chosen take of the chair, the one we used. Below it, the line: TEN THOUSAND TAKES. ONE HIT.
Motion
Tiles converge with exponential ease-out over one second. The survivor scales to full frame. The counter runs down from 10,000 to 1.
On screen
TEN THOUSAND TAKES. ONE HIT.
Audio
The pulse cuts. Silence, then the steel ring is suddenly audible again.
AI
Curation is the human beat. An AI ranking pass surfaces candidates and the studio picks. The piece says this out loud: the machine makes ten thousand, the studio makes the call.
Reference
A paraphrase of the ten-thousand-kicks idea in the studio's own words. No quotation.
Out
Smash to black with the ruler already in place.

The lockup

Visual
Black. The ruler from frame 02 runs across center. The One Inch Media lockup slams in and lands so that the wordmark spans exactly one inch on the ruler: the 0 and 1 marks sit on its edges. One shockwave ring on the landing. Readout: 1.000 in.

The lockup arrives at 1.6× and settles to 1× with a hard overshoot. Two-frame screen shake on contact. The ring expands and fades.

Procedural type animation derived from the actual logo geometry (strokes, counters), so the slam-in is built from the mark itself. Physically based materials on the lockup matched to the brand kit.

What the piece proves about AI

One capability per beat, in the order a technically literate viewer would clock them. Nothing is decoration: each shot would not exist without the technique that made it.

  • F01Generative sound design matched to a described or scanned room.
  • F02Photoreal text-to-video with numerically specified camera paths.
  • F03Temporal super-resolution: 1000 fps-equivalent slow motion from 24 fps.
  • F04Neural physics: shock through steel with real material properties.
  • F05Object-controlled generative video inside a Gaussian-splat capture of a real room.
  • F06Image-to-video, 2D-to-3D lift, text-to-speech, wireframe-to-photoreal, in one continuous shot.
  • F07Batch generation at scale with seeded variation and automatic reframing; live rendering on the web.
  • F08AI-assisted ranking, human curation. The studio's role, stated.
  • F09Procedural type animation derived from the logo's own geometry.
  • F10Voice synthesis and generated spectral audio tails.

Sound

Near silence, room tone, one dry hit. A long metallic ring from a struck steel plate decays under the whole second half. A dry pulse builds under the ten thousand and cuts dead. One synthesized voice speaks twice. No gong, no film cue, no vocalization, no sample from any Bruce Lee film. The score is generated from a tempo map locked to the cuts.

Guardrails and your calls

What the piece deliberately avoids so the homage is safe to run commercially, and the few decisions only you can make.

What we do not do

  • No likeness. No face, body, silhouette or costume of Bruce Lee, in any frame, including as a reflection or shadow.
  • No name. Not on screen, not in the voice track, not in metadata.
  • No registered phrases. "Be water, my friend" is a registered mark and is not used. Neither line on screen is a quotation; both are paraphrases in the studio's own words.
  • No trade dress. Yellow appears only as a flat accent. No black stripe, no tracksuit, no nunchaku.
  • No film material. No footage, stills, score or vocalizations from any film. The chair references a public demonstration, not a film scene.
  • Original audio only. The score and every effect are generated or recorded for this piece.

Decisions for you

  1. Confirm the brand is One Inch Media and share the kit: logo, colors, fonts. Everything in Brand slots then drops in.
  2. Pick the line. "One inch. Total impact." is the proposal. Alternates: "Everything from one inch." or no tagline, ending on the lockup and the ruler.
  3. The voice. Your own voice cloned (consent and a short recording), a cast synthetic voice, or no voice at all.
  4. The chair. Keep the deep-cut nod, or make the hit land on something abstract. It is the one beat that is recognizable only to people who know the story.
  5. The real room. Scan your studio for frames 05 to 07, or let it be generated.
  6. Live variation on the web. Each visitor sees a different ten thousand, or everyone sees one fixed render.

Other directions considered

Five angles that lost to the inch, and what each one still lends to the board.

Formless

A fluid taking the shape of every container: phone, billboard, cinema, letterform. Strong idea, but the registered phrase is the first thing a viewer reaches for. It survives as the mixed aspect ratios in frame 07.

The mirror room

Infinite reflections, which one is real, shatter the rest. Spectacular but it belongs to one film scene. It survives as the ten thousand tiles going dark around the one in frame 08.

Ten thousand

Iteration at scale converging on one perfect take. Too much of a montage on its own, and it does not pay off the name. It became the third movement instead.

Style of no style

Every medium absorbed into one fluid motion, render technique changing mid-shot. Rich, hard to read in thirty seconds. It survives as the continuous dolly in frame 06.

The ascent

Floors of increasing difficulty, each a different medium, climbed without a cut. A great structure for a longer film, but the vertical climb never arrives at an inch. Set aside.
