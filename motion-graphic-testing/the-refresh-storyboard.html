The Refresh

A forty-second war in stick figures: a brand holds a line, time attacks every day, and a refresh is the reinforcements that arrive. Nobody dies. The stale get refreshed.

  • 40 s
  • 16:9 · 24 fps
  • 10 frames
  • 8 s cutdown
  • Stick figures, big action, no blood
  • Built on the real lockup
  • Agent 1 leads

The argument

  • A brand is a line held against time. Time attacks every day and never stops: trends, taste, new rivals, platforms, algorithms. The horde in this film carries those names on its flags, and the year counter never runs backward.
  • A brand that never refreshes fights with old assets. The defenders throw GIFs, Flash files and clip art off the wall. They bounce. The wall cracks and the flag tears. That is what "we'll update the site someday" looks like from above.
  • A refresh is not surrender. It is reinforcements. Agent 1 arrives on the hill with the red flag and an army. The slash sweeps the field, and every stale figure it touches is refreshed, not felled. The wall is rebuilt. The new flag goes up. The line moves forward.
  • Why stick figures. They let the film be enormous and funny at the same time, and they make the one real figure, your numeral, the hero by contrast. A hundred-figure clash in stick figures is a weekend, not a quarter.

The armies

Two kinds of stick figure and one rule. The loop shows both, and what the wave does to the stale one.

  • StaleHollow head, grey, identical. Walks, never runs. Carries a flag with a year or a threat on it. Slumps when it stands still.
  • FreshFilled head, bone, varied. Runs with a 20° lean and pumping arms. Carries the red flag with the mark. Raises both arms when it wins.
  • The ruleNobody falls. When the slash passes a stale figure it fills in, turns bone, and raises its arms. The war converts; it does not kill. That is the whole argument in one gesture.
  • Agent 1The hero is your numeral, the same rig as ONE SHOT: legs, an arm, the lean. It is the only figure that is not a stick, so the eye finds it in any crowd.
  • FlagsThe old flag is a pixel grid, torn at the trailing edge, with EST. 2014 under it. The new flag is red with the mark. The film is about which one flies at the end.
  • WeaponsThere are none. Defenders throw old assets. The horde brings ladders. The reinforcements bring a catapult that launches the mark. The strike is the slash.

The fort

Visual
Night. A stick-figure fort drawn in bone line on a hill: a cracked wall with crenellations, a tower, and on the tower a flag that is a pixel grid, torn at the trailing edge. Under it: EST. 2014. Six grey defenders slump on the wall, heads nodding. Stars. Top of frame: EVERY BRAND HOLDS A LINE.
Motion
Slow push-in, 1.0× to 1.08×. The flag waves at a tired half speed. One defender nods on a four-second cycle.
Audio
Wind. A snore. A single low drum, once.
AI
Generative environment: the fort and hill are a 3D set generated from one line sketch in the brand's stroke weight, lit for night. The pixel flag is the client's actual old logo, downsampled.
Out
A rumble. Cut wide.
020:04 – 0:08 · 4 s

The dust

Visual
Wide. The fort small at left. On the right horizon a dust cloud grows until it fills a third of the sky, and inside it, tiny silhouettes, more every second, some with flags. The ground begins to shake. Lower right, in the mark's serif, a year counter climbs: 2014 to 2026.
Motion
Locked wide with a shake that grows from nothing to three pixels. The cloud expands on an ease-out; the silhouettes multiply on an ease-in.
Audio
A rumble that becomes marching feet. A tick per year.
AI
Crowd simulation seeded at scale: the silhouettes are the first thousand agents of the horde, already walking, seen from a mile off. Volumetric dust driven by their footfalls.
Out
Hard cut into the horde.
030:08 – 0:12 · 4 s

The horde

Visual
Low angle, in the march. Row after row of grey hollow-headed figures walking toward the fort, the near ones cropped by the frame. A third carry flags: TRENDS, 2021, ALGORITHM, NEW RIVAL, 2024, TASTE, PLATFORMS. Dust at their feet. Top of frame: TIME DOESN'T STOP ATTACKING.
Motion
Six rows at six depths, the far rows slower. Every figure walks the same cycle, deliberately. Camera tracks with them at knee height.
Audio
Marching in unison. Flags snapping. The drum returns, steady.
AI
Crowd simulation with one shared walk cycle, offset per agent, so a thousand figures read as one organism. The flag labels are generated from the client's actual market: real competitor categories, real platform shifts, real years.
Out
The wall rises into frame on the left.
040:12 – 0:16 · 4 s

The siege

Visual
At the wall. Five defenders on top throw what they have: little grey boxes labeled GIF, FLASH, LOREM, .PSD, CLIPART, POP-UP. The boxes arc, bounce off the horde and drop. Ladders go up. Cracks spread across the wall. The pixel flag sheds squares.
Motion
Boxes on a 1.6-second arc each, staggered. Ladders rise on three beats. Cracks grow on an ease-in; the shake is a one-pixel kick per crack.
Audio
Six comic bounces. Wood on stone. A long crack.
AI
Rigid-body simulation for the thrown assets and the falling pixels; a procedural fracture on the wall; the labels on the boxes pulled from an audit of the client's actual asset library, so the joke is specific.
Out
Whip pan right, to the hill.
050:16 – 0:20 · 4 s

The hill

Visual
A ridge against a rising red sun. Agent 1 lands on the crest, your numeral with legs, and plants a pole: the red flag with the mark. Behind the ridge, heads appear, then shoulders, then a whole army of bone figures rising into view, some with red flags. Left of frame, large: REFRESH.
Motion
Agent 1 arrives with overshoot. The army rises over two seconds on an ease-out. The flag catches wind. The sun climbs a tenth of the frame.
Audio
Silence, then one hit when the flag plants. A rising swell as the army appears.
AI
The reveal is a crowd of two thousand agents placed behind a terrain mesh and raised on a curve. The sun and the flag's cloth are simulated. The hero rig is the ONE SHOT character, reused.
Out
Agent 1 raises the slash. Cut to the charge.
060:20 – 0:24 · 4 s

The charge

Visual
Downhill, full sprint. Five rows of bone figures running with a hard forward lean, arms pumping, red flags streaming. Agent 1 out in front with the slash held low. Speed lines from the right edge, dust behind every foot. The horde turning to meet them at the far left.
Motion
Run cycles at 2.2 strides a second, the near row crossing the frame in under a second. Camera tracks at a slight low angle.
Audio
Footfalls become a roar. The drum doubles. A battle cry that is just a thousand voices saying one word: refresh.
AI
Crowd simulation with a run cycle from a motion prior, per-agent variation in stride and lean, and terrain-aware footing on the slope. Dust and speed lines as particle systems.
Out
Impact.
070:24 – 0:28 · 4 s

The clash

Visual
The two crowds meet. The slash sweeps the field as a red wave from right to left, and every grey figure it passes fills in, turns bone and throws both arms up. Behind the line a catapult fires: a bone tile with the mark on it arcs across the whole frame and lands in the middle of the field in a puff of dust.
Motion
The wave crosses in three seconds on an ease in and out. Refreshed figures bounce. The catapult arm fires in four frames; the tile flies for two seconds.
Audio
The hit, held and stretched into a wave. Cheers rising behind it. A thunk when the tile lands.
AI
Per-agent state driven by the wave: a thousand simultaneous transitions from one rig state to another, with the jump cycle generated so no two look alike. The catapult and tile are rigid-body physics.
Out
Cut to the wall.
080:28 – 0:32 · 4 s

The rebuild

Visual
The wall rebuilt in a sweep: blocks snap in from the bottom row to the top, crenellations last. On the pole, the pixel flag drops and the red flag runs up. Fifty bone figures stand around it with their arms up. Top of frame: AND THE LINE MOVES FORWARD.
Motion
Blocks on an ease-out over 2.4 seconds. The old flag falls in eight frames; the new one rises in ten.
Audio
Stone on stone, fast. A flag snapping. The drum resolves.
AI
Procedural construction: the wall is rebuilt from the same generated set, block by block, as an animation of the model itself. The new flag is the real lockup, cloth-simulated.

Guardrails and your calls

A war film for a brand has one rule: nobody is hurt and nothing real is referenced. Then the decisions.

What we do not do

  • No weapons. Flags, ladders, thrown assets and a catapult that fires a logo. The strike is the slash.
  • Nobody falls. The stale are refreshed, not defeated. Every figure that starts the film is standing at dawn.
  • No blood, no fire, no real war. No reference to any battle, army, uniform or conflict, historical or fictional.
  • No real brands on the horde's flags. Categories and years only. The thrown assets name file types, not products.
  • No library battle audio. Everything generated or recorded for this piece.
  • The stale look stays off the brand. Grey, hollow and pixelated belong to the past; nothing of yours is drawn that way.

Decisions for you

  1. The four lines. "Every brand holds a line." "Time doesn't stop attacking." "Refresh." "And the line moves forward." Keep, cut, or replace with a single voice-over.
  2. Nobody dies. The board converts the stale instead of felling them. A harsher version is possible and the board's answer is no.
  3. The year counter. 2014 to 2026 is the proposal. A client version uses their founding year.
  4. Specific or generic. Flags and thrown assets named from a real client's market and files, or kept generic for the studio reel.
  5. The service line. "Brand refresh" under the lockup, or nothing.
  6. The voice. One line at dawn in your voice, a cast voice, or none.

Other directions considered

Three other ways to fight this war that lost to the fort.

The duel

One stale figure against one fresh one, in close-up. Clear, but small, and the brief said epic. It survives as the two figures in the armies loop above.

The march

No battle at all: a parade of every version a brand has ever been, oldest first, ending with the current one. Warm, but it argues for history, not for change.

The last stand

The fort falls completely and the brand rebuilds from nothing. Dramatic, but it tells a client that it is already too late, which is not the message. The board keeps the wall standing and reinforces it.
