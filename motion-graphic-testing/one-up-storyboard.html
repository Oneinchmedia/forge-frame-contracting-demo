One Up Intro Storyboard

Storyboard · brand ident · One Inch Media · companion to ONE INCH

One Up

A thirty-second ident that sends the 1 up a five-floor tower, one strike per floor, and out through the aperture at the top.

  • 30 s
  • 16:9 · 24 fps
  • 10 frames
  • 7 s cutdown
  • Bruce Lee homage, no likeness
  • Built on the real lockup
  • 128 bpm, loud

How this differs from ONE INCH

  • Same brand devices, opposite energy. ONE INCH is stillness and one hit. ONE UP is a sprint: five floors, five strikes, a beat the whole way, and the mark as the finish line. The serif 1 from your lockup is the hero; the slash is its strike; the aperture is the exit.
  • Five references, one per floor. Boards that don't hit back, water that takes any shape, one kick ten thousand times, the mirror room, and the climb itself. All by image and paraphrase, no likeness, no quotes, no footage.
  • Both boards end on the same lockup and line, so they can run as a pair: ONE INCH for the site and cinema, ONE UP for reels and pre-rolls.
  • Still open: vector logo files, the fonts, a scan of the studio, and the voice. Type here is matched by stand-ins.

Structure

Ground, three fast floors, one slow floor, the top. The beat never stops until the leap, and the silence after it is the only rest in the piece.

Ground

The tower at night, the 1 at the door, and a whip up to the first floor plate.

Floors 1 to 3

The board

Ten frames. Each names the AI capability it proves and how the shot would be produced. Timings sum to thirty seconds exactly.

010:00 – 0:02 · 2 s

The tower

Visual
Night. A five-tier tower drawn in a single bone line on black, eaves stacked, one doorway at the base glowing red. Small at the door: the 1 from the mark, standing. Floor plates beside each tier, 1 to 5, in the mark's serif, unlit.
Motion
The camera tilts up the whole tower in two seconds, ease-in, so the height registers, then snaps back to the door.
On screen
The floor plates, unlit.
Audio
One kick drum. Wind. A riser under the tilt.
AI
Text-to-3D: the tower is a generated asset from one sketch and the brand's line weight, rendered at night with the doorway as the only light. The score is generated from a tempo map at 128 bpm, one accent per floor.
Reference
The climb, by structure only. A stylized tower in the brand's line, not the film's temple.
Out
Whip up into the doorway.
020:02 – 0:04 · 2 s

One up

Visual
Whip up through the door into the tower. Vertical streaks of light. A red floor plate slams in with the serif 1: floor one. The 1 lands in frame, leaning forward like it is already running.
Motion
Speed ramp from 400% to 100% as the plate lands. Two-frame hold on the plate.
On screen
Floor plate 1.
Audio
Whip. A snare on the plate. The beat starts.
AI
Camera-path control and frame synthesis for the speed ramp: the whip is generated between two keyframes, not shot. The 1 is a procedural character animated from the mark's own vector outline, so it is always the real numeral.
Out
Hard cut on the beat.
030:04 – 0:08 · 4 s

Floor 1 · Boards

Visual
A corridor stacked with boards: posters, magazine spreads, billboards, a bus side, a storefront window, all blank bone rectangles in a dozen aspect ratios. The 1 dashes left to right. The slash follows and every board splits clean in two, the halves peeling apart to reveal the same campaign generated at that size. Lower left, small: NOTHING HITS BACK.
Motion
One lateral dolly matching the run. Boards split in sequence, each on a beat subdivision. One-frame camera shake per split.
On screen
NOTHING HITS BACK. (your call) and floor plate 1.
Audio
Beat. A dry crack per board, pitched higher as the boards get smaller.
AI
Generative print at every size: one brief rendered to twelve formats with correct composition for each (poster, spread, billboard, bus side) in a single pass. The splits are rigid-body simulation, not animated by hand.
Reference
A paraphrase of the "boards don't hit back" line in the studio's own words. No quotation.
Out
The last board is a doorway; the 1 goes through, whip up.
040:08 – 0:12 · 4 s

Floor 2 · Water

Visual
A showroom of empty screens: a phone, a laptop, a billboard, a watch. The 1 pours. It becomes liquid, fills the phone to its exact edges, drains, fills the laptop, the billboard, the watch, each time keeping the mark's proportions. Red ripples where it lands.
Motion
Continuous, no cuts. The liquid moves on real fluid physics; the camera arcs around the room.
On screen
Floor plate 2.
Audio
Beat under water: pours, a splash per screen, the hats as drips.
AI
Neural fluid simulation driving automatic reframing: one asset adapting to every device without a person re-cutting it. The showcase is that the mark never breaks; the 1 stays recognizable as liquid.
Reference
The formless idea, by image only. No phrase on screen.
Out
The liquid climbs the far wall and lifts the camera to floor three.
050:12 – 0:16 · 4 s

Floor 3 · Ten thousand

Visual
A training hall. The 1 strikes once, a lean and a slash, and the strike repeats, and repeats. The hall fills with 1s mid-strike, every one at a slightly different angle, until ten thousand stand in a grid. Then, in one frame, every one snaps to the same angle: one strike, perfected. Counter in the serif.
Motion
Time-lapse; the grid fills at an accelerating rate; the snap is a single frame; the camera pulls back to show the whole hall.
On screen
Floor plate 3 and the counter, 10,000 then 1.
Audio
The beat doubles. Ten thousand strikes rolled into one drum fill. Silence on the snap.
AI
Batch generation of ten thousand variants of one motion, time-lapsed, then converged by a ranking pass. The one-kick-ten-thousand-times idea as a literal production method.
Reference
A paraphrase of the ten-thousand-kicks idea. No quotation.
Out
Whip up on the silence.
060:16 – 0:21 · 5 s

Floor 4 · Mirrors

Visual
A room of mirrors. The 1 is reflected a hundred times, every reflection a slightly different render: another light, another lens, another finish. One is real. The 1 strikes once; the slash runs through every mirror at the same instant and the glass goes, shards flying past camera, leaving the one real 1 standing in a clean room.
Motion
Slow orbit while the reflections multiply, then one strike and a shatter at slow motion for twelve frames, then back to speed.
On screen
Floor plate 4.
Audio
The beat drops to a pulse. One strike. Glass, slowed. The pulse returns.
AI
Generative variants with a ranking pass, shown as reflections; the studio's choice is the strike. The shatter is a fracture simulation with glass refraction, rendered, not stock.
Reference
The mirror room, by image only. No footage, no set reproduction.
Out
The 1 looks up. Red light from above.
070:21 – 0:24 · 3 s

Floor 5 · The aperture

Visual
Straight up from the floor. The ceiling is the mark's aperture: six steel blades, open, red light pouring through. The 1 leaps, straight at camera and through the opening, blades on every side.
Motion
Low angle, locked. The 1 rises with exponential ease-in. Speed lines. A whip through the opening into white.
On screen
Floor plate 5.
Audio
Riser. The beat cuts out on the leap. A shutter opens.
AI
Real-time 3D in a game engine with a procedural camera, so the web version can be interactive: the viewer triggers the leap. The blades are the logo's own vectors, extruded and given brushed steel.
Out
Through the aperture into white.

The lockup

Visual
White to black. The six blades rotate in from six directions and lock around the 1, which is now the serif 1 of the mark at rest. The slash cuts through on the hit. ONE INCH tracks out beneath; MEDIA resolves in red. One ring from the mark's center.
Motion
Blades arrive with overshoot; the slash is one frame; the type tracks out over eight frames. In the previz the blades converge procedurally and cross-fade to the real lockup.
On screen
The lockup.

AI
Procedural animation from the logo vectors: blades, numeral, slash and letters built from the mark itself. Physically based materials: brushed steel blades, ink type, the red as emissive.

What the piece proves about AI

One capability per floor, in the order a technically literate viewer would clock them. Each shot would not exist without the technique that made it.

  • F01Text-to-3D: a whole environment from one sketch, plus a tempo-mapped generated score.
  • F02Frame synthesis for speed ramps; a procedural character from the mark's vectors.
  • F03Generative print at every size in one pass; rigid-body simulation for the splits.
  • F04Neural fluid simulation driving automatic reframing across devices.
  • F05Batch generation of ten thousand variants, converged by a ranking pass.
  • F06Variants and curation as reflections; a fracture simulation with refraction.
  • F07Real-time 3D in a game engine, so the web version can be interactive.
  • F08Procedural animation from the logo vectors with physically based materials.
  • F09Voice synthesis.
  • F10Automatic end-cards for every aspect ratio with the line safe-framed.

The floor plates and the counter use the mark's serif, so the numbers are always the brand's numbers. The single spoken line is set like the wordmark. The lockup itself is the artwork, never re-set.

Sound

A kinetic 128 bpm beat built from one kick, one snare and hats, generated from a tempo map locked to the cuts. One accent per floor: cracks, splashes, a drum fill, glass, a riser. The beat cuts out on the leap and never comes back; the lockup lands in silence with one hit and the ring. A shutter click closes it. No gong, no film cue, no vocalization, no sample from any Bruce Lee film.

Guardrails and your calls

What the piece deliberately avoids so the homage is safe to run commercially, and the few decisions only you can make.

What we do not do

  • No likeness. No face, body, silhouette or costume of Bruce Lee, in any frame. The hero is your numeral.
  • No name. Not on screen, not in the voice track, not in metadata.
  • No quotes. "Nothing hits back" and the ten-thousand counter are paraphrases. "Be water, my friend" is a registered mark and is not used; the water floor has no words.
  • No trade dress. Your black, white and red. No yellow, no stripe, no tracksuit, no nunchaku.
  • No film sets. The tower is a stylized line drawing in the brand's line, not the film's temple. The mirror room is a room of mirrors, not a reproduction of the set.
  • Original audio only. The score and every effect are generated or recorded for this piece.

Decisions for you

  1. "Nothing hits back." Keep it on floor one, or let the boards speak for themselves. It is the one line that leans on a famous one.
  2. Game or film. A level-clear tone on each floor plate makes it a game; dropping it keeps it a film. The board assumes film.
  3. The button. End on the shut aperture and the slash flash, or hold on the lockup like ONE INCH does.
  4. The voice. Your own voice cloned (consent and a short recording), a cast synthetic voice, or none.
  5. Interactive on the web. Let the viewer trigger the leap, or play it straight.
  6. Which runs where. ONE INCH for the site and cinema, ONE UP for reels and pre-rolls, or either everywhere.

Other fun directions considered

Three angles that lost to the tower, and what each one still lends.

The dojo

A training montage: the 1 drills one move against a wall of media formats, faster each rep. Energetic, but it is one floor of the tower stretched to thirty seconds. It became floor three.

Fighting without fighting

Every obstacle steps aside before the 1 has to strike; the piece is one clean walk. Smart, and very on-brand for a studio that wins on detail, but hard to make dynamic. It survives in the tower's rule: one strike per floor, never a brawl.

The intercepting fist

The 1 intercepts incoming noise mid-flight: bad ads, bad renders, bad type, cut down one by one. Fun, but it spends the whole piece on other people's work. The slash-through-every-mirror beat on floor four keeps the best of it.
