One Shot

A twelve-second logo opening: the dots, a barrel that is really your aperture, a 1 that walks, turns and fires the slash, the red that falls, and an iris that closes into your mark.

  • 12 s
  • 16:9 · 24 fps
  • 6 frames
  • 4 s sting
  • 007 homage, no likeness, no gun
  • Built on the real lockup
  • Character: Agent 1

How this one works

  • Every element of the famous opening is replaced with one of yours. The barrel is the aperture, six straight blades repeated into a tunnel. The figure is the serif 1 from the mark, given legs and an arm. The shot is the slash. The blood is the brand red. The iris that closes at the end is the mark itself, and the 1 walks into the middle of it.
  • It says what you do. At the far end of the barrel sits a tired template website. Agent 1 walks the barrel to deal with it, turns, fires, and the red takes the old site with it. The end card can carry a service line under the lockup.
  • The character is the point. Agent 1 is rigged from the logo's own numeral: a walk with lean and bob, a turn that flips through the stem, an arm that comes up, a strike that is the slash. The loop below shows the cycle.
  • Still open: whether the shot is ever a literal pistol (the board says no), the service line, fonts and voice. Type here is matched by stand-ins.

Agent 1

The character, rigged from the mark's numeral. This loop runs the three moves the opening needs: the walk, the turn, the fire.

  • BodyThe serif 1, untouched. The base serif reads as the hips; the flag reads as a head. Nothing is redrawn, so the character is always the real mark.
  • LegsTwo-segment legs under the base, with a knee that bends on the swing and a foot that points the way it walks. Stride 1.6 steps a second, a 6° forward lean, a 3.5% bob.
  • ArmOne arm at the shoulder, swinging opposite the legs. It rises to horizontal in eight frames for the fire.
  • TurnA flip through the stem: the numeral scales through zero and comes back facing the other way, seventeen frames, with the walk dying out underneath.
  • FireThe slash is the shot. A red glow at the hand, then the brand's diagonal grows from the hand to fill the frame in five frames. No pistol.
  • SettleLegs retract, arm drops, and the character becomes the numeral in the lockup. The walk-in at the end is the rig returning to being a logo.

In production the rig is built from the logo vector: the stem and base serif as the parent, legs and arm as children, with the same timings as the previz. A motion prior tuned on hand-animated walk cycles gives the stride its weight.

010:00 – 0:02 · 2 s

The dots

Visual
Black. Six bone dots travel left to right across the center line, evenly spaced. The last one stops at center and opens into a small six-sided iris with steel inside.
Motion
Constant speed, no easing, the way a shutter chain moves. The opening takes eight frames.
On screen
None.
Audio
A camera shutter per dot, six clicks. A single low bass note starts on the sixth and holds.
AI
Generative sound design: six real shutter recordings, pitched and timed by an agent to the dot spacing, so the clicks are the rhythm track.
Reference
The traveling dots, by rhythm. Ours are shutter clicks, not a drum.
Out
The iris keeps opening into frame 02.
020:02 – 0:05 · 3 s

The barrel

Visual
The iris opens to a view down a tunnel made of the aperture repeated in depth: six straight steel blades, ring after ring, slowly turning. At the far end, small and grey: a template website. Agent 1 enters from the right and walks left along the floor of the tunnel, and the iris tracks it.
Motion
The iris opens over 18 frames. The walk is 1.6 steps a second with lean and bob. The rings turn a quarter-turn across the shot. The iris pans with the character.
On screen
None.
Audio
Footsteps on steel, every step a different ring. The bass note holds.
AI
Procedural geometry from the mark: the tunnel is the logo's own blade shape instanced into depth. The character is rigged from the logo vector and walked by a motion prior, so no one keyframes a stride.
Reference
The barrel, by composition. No rifling, no spiral, no gun metal: the aperture's straight blades, which look like nothing else.
Out
Agent 1 reaches center and stops.
030:05 – 0:06.5 · 1.5 s

The turn

Visual
The walk dies out. Agent 1 turns to camera: the numeral flips through its own stem and comes back facing us. The arm comes up to horizontal. The iris tightens slightly on it.
Motion
Settle in seven frames, flip in seventeen, arm up in eight. The iris closes by 8%.
On screen
None.
Audio
Footsteps stop. The bass note swells. Silence for the last six frames.
AI
The turn is the hardest beat for a flat character; a generated in-between pass produces the squash through the stem and the secondary motion on the legs and arm, from two drawn poses.
Reference
The turn and the raise, by timing. No silhouette of a man, no hat, no suit.
Out
Hold one frame on the raised arm.
040:06.5 – 0:07.5 · 1 s

The shot

Visual
A red glow at the hand, then the slash: the brand's diagonal grows from the hand to fill the frame. One frame of bone flash. The frame kicks. The old site at the end of the tunnel is gone.
Motion
Glow in two frames, slash across in five, a seven-frame shake that decays. Agent 1 leans back 5° on the recoil and returns.
On screen
None.
Audio
One dry snap, no reverb. The ring from the other films starts under it.
AI
The slash as a volumetric light simulation, rendered, so it has depth inside the tunnel instead of sitting on top of the frame.
Reference
The shot, by consequence only. There is no gun in the film. The slash is what this brand fires.
Out
Red begins at the top of frame.
050:07.5 – 0:09.5 · 2 s

The red

Visual
The brand red falls from the top edge and covers everything, the barrel, the character, the old site, with a dripping lower edge that reads as liquid without being gore. On solid red, the iris closes: six blades turn and shut to black.
Motion
The red accelerates as it falls, 30 frames. The iris closes in 14 frames with a slow rotation.
On screen
None.
Audio
A swell as the red falls. A shutter click on the close. The ring holds.
AI
Fluid simulation for the falling edge, tuned to read as paint rather than blood: higher viscosity, no spatter. The iris is the logo geometry again.
Reference
The red wash, by motion. A clean wipe in the brand color, not blood.
Out
Black.
060:09.5 – 0:12 · 2.5 s

The mark

Visual
Out of black, the six blades of the mark rotate in and lock into the ring. Agent 1 walks in from the right, steps into the center of the ring, the walk settles, the legs retract, the arm drops: it is the 1 of the logo now. The slash cuts through. ONE INCH and MEDIA resolve, the line wipes in beneath, and under it, small and red: WEBSITE REDESIGN.
Motion
Blades with overshoot over ten frames; a one-second walk-in; the settle in six frames; the slash in one; the type over eight; the wipe over twelve.
On screen
The lockup, SMALL DETAILS. BIG IMPACT. and the service line.
Audio
Two last footsteps. The hit. The ring decays to silence.
AI
Procedural animation from the logo vectors, shared with the other three films, plus the rig returning to rest. End-cards generated for every aspect ratio with the service line safe-framed.
Reference
Where the original cuts to a title, ours becomes the mark. The character walking into the logo is the whole idea.
Out
Hold, then black.

Guardrails and your calls

The gun barrel opening is one of the most protected sequences in film. This is how the homage stays an homage.

What we do not do

  • No gun. Nothing in the film is a pistol. The shot is the slash.
  • No man. No silhouette, no tuxedo, no hat, no walk that imitates a person. The character is a numeral with legs.
  • No rifling. The barrel is the aperture: straight blades, no spiral grooves.
  • No blood. The red is a clean wipe in the brand color with a paint edge, and it reads as a wipe.
  • No theme, no numerals, no title. No riff, no brass, no "007", no gun logo, no "will return" card.
  • Original audio only. Shutters, footsteps, one bass note, the hit, the ring.

Decisions for you

  1. Pistol or slash. The board fires the slash. A literal pistol is where the homage becomes a copy and where a web studio's logo carries a weapon. Your call, and the board's answer is no.
  2. The service line. "Website redesign" under the lockup, a different line, or nothing.
  3. The old site in the barrel. Keep it so the opening says what you do, or keep the barrel clean.
  4. The walk. The lean-and-bob in the loop above has character. A straighter, cooler walk is a different personality. Watch the loop and pick.
  5. The full and the sting. Twelve seconds for the site and pitches, four for everything else.

Other directions considered

Two other ways into the same world that lost to the barrel.

The briefing

Agent 1 gets its assignment: a dossier on the old site, the gadgets of a redesign laid out on a desk. Charming, but it is a scene, not an opening, and it runs long. The old site at the end of the barrel keeps the idea in one glance.

The titles

A title-sequence treatment with silhouettes and liquid shapes. The famous version of that trope is one the brand should not go near, and without it the idea has no spine. Set aside.
